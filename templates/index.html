Under the hood

How the scan works

No page ever has to load. The whole decision is made from the shape of the URL string itself, which is why it works even on brand-new phishing domains.

01
Extract features

~25 signals are pulled from the URL: length, symbol counts, entropy, IP-as-host, suspicious keywords, brand impersonation in the subdomain, and more.

02
Score with ML

Five models (Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, Decision Tree, Logistic Regression, SVM) are compared at training time; the best performer by F1 score is kept.

03
Explain the verdict

Alongside the probability, the specific red or green flags that drove the score — like a missing HTTPS or an IP-address host — are surfaced in plain language.

Background

What is phishing, exactly?

Phishing is a social-engineering attack where someone impersonates a trusted person, brand, or service — usually by email, text, or a fake website — to trick a victim into handing over credentials, payment details, or access to a system.

It doesn't rely on breaking encryption or exploiting software bugs. It relies on human trust and urgency: a login page that looks exactly like your bank's, a message claiming your account will be suspended in an hour, a link from what looks like a coworker.

Because the barrier to entry is low and the payoff is high, phishing remains one of the most common ways attackers get an initial foothold — into personal accounts and into companies.

Mismatched or lookalike domainpaypal-secure-login.com is not paypal.com.
Urgency or fear"Your account will be locked in 24 hours" is designed to stop you from thinking.
Requests for credentialsLegitimate services rarely ask you to "confirm your password" via a link.
Odd sender or IP-based linksAn address as the host, or a domain that doesn't match the sender, is a strong signal.
📧

Email phishing

Mass, generic messages impersonating a brand, sent to as many inboxes as possible.

🎯

Spear phishing

Targeted at one person or company using details researched about them specifically.

💬

Smishing

The same trick delivered by SMS text message, often with a shortened link.

☎️

Vishing

A phone call impersonating support staff or a bank to extract information verbally.

🪞

Clone phishing

A near-identical copy of a real email or site, with the link swapped for a malicious one.

🎣

Whaling

Spear phishing aimed at executives, often to authorize a fraudulent payment or transfer.

🌐

Pharming

Redirecting a legitimate-looking address to a fake site at the DNS level, not the link.

🔗

Link manipulation

Using shorteners, @ symbols, or lookalike characters so the real destination is hidden.
